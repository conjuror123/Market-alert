Since the last report card

The detector was reading the first five minutes of every hour

Of everything found since the previous card, one fault stands out — not because it was subtle, but because this page could not see it. Every figure below is measured on a cold pass over complete bars. The bot was running on something else.

Every hour was judged on its opening minutes

The hourly job fires five minutes past the hour and stored a bar for the hour it was standing in — measured on the committed store, 2–13% of that hour's volume. The bars healed on the next fetch; the metrics did not, because the incremental path only ever computed hours newer than the last one it had. The complete bar arrived to find its hour already written.

So the damage landed exactly where it hurts: on a quiet hour a fifth of the move and the whole move both fail to be records, and nothing changes. On a news hour they differ by enough to cross the threshold. 16 September, 18:00 UTC — the FOMC statement — went into the store as SHY +0.02% when the hour had closed at -0.19%, six sigma. Thirteen instruments' events went with it: bonds, gold and the dollar all moving together, and not one of them reached the phone that day.

None of the numbers on this page moved

That is the uncomfortable part, and worth stating plainly: recall , false alarms , pushes over years. Identical to the last report card, because a backtest is a cold pass over complete bars — which is precisely what the fix restores. The backtest was measuring a detector better than the one that was running, and nothing compared the two.

A dashboard that reads 97.2% while the live bot misses an FOMC hour is not wrong about the detector; it is answering a question nobody should have been satisfied with. The gap between "what the code would do over history" and "what the deployed thing did last Wednesday" had no measurement at all. It still does not — that is the honest state, and the next thing worth building.

Detection quality

Did it catch the obvious, did it stay quiet on the rest

Two tests, neither with a threshold anyone can argue about. The bucket is each instrument's own return percentile, not a shared percentage, so the ladder is not grading its own homework.

Where the bars come from

Three feeds, chosen per instrument by measurement

The basket used to come from one provider whose free tier forces an eight-second pause between symbols. That pause was the run. Two faster feeds replaced it — but only where each was shown to price the instrument the same way, hour by hour, against the bars already stored.

Disagreement with the stored bars, in basis points

Median difference on the hourly close over the same 62 hours. One sigma of an hourly move is 20–40 bps for these instruments, so 2 bps is under a tenth of a sigma and invisible; 10 bps starts to move the ladder on its own.

Why the thin funds did not move

One provider serves a single exchange holding about 5.5% of consolidated volume. On liquid funds that is the same price to a fifth of a basis point. On thin single-commodity funds it is a different price — and a different price is an alert for a move that never happened, which is the one failure worth paying to avoid. Those fifteen stayed on a consolidated feed.

The archive provider remained configured and still held the history: neither fast feed can reach back — one serves 55 days of half-hourly bars, the other caps a response at 10,000 rows. The identity of an instrument never moved with its feed, so no stored bar, no recorded verdict and no exported event was orphaned by the change.

What it could not do, as of this snapshot

Open at the date above. Unlike everything else on the page these are not measured, so they are the first thing to go stale — check them against docs/operations.md before relying on one.

Nothing notices a silence

The health check counts consecutive failures, and only a run that executes can increment one. When the trigger stopped for six days, nothing in the repository could say so. A watchdog is designed and not yet built.

Thirteen instruments stop at 2020

A provider plan limit, not a fault. The dividend fix removes the blocker on five of them; the deepening run itself has not been done. XLP reaches only 2022 for a reason nobody has established.

One feed is undocumented

The consolidated feed serving fifteen thin funds is an unversioned endpoint with no support channel. It can change shape without notice. That is why the basket is split across two providers rather than sent to one.

The feed test is one week long

Sixty-two overlapping hours decided which provider serves what. A volatile week could read differently. The comparison re-runs in forty-four requests and should, periodically.

It does not predict

And does not claim to. Every number here is about a move that already happened; the tier says how unusual it was, not what comes next.

Retention is a note, not a gate

A move that fully reverts still interrupted you. of pushes had given everything back by the next close — the message says so afterwards, which is all it can do.
